# ECGrav 1.10.1 — four defects in the CTL schedule builder, all invisible at one external field

`GraphCTLSchedule` places parallel-tempering replicas at constant thermodynamic length. Four things
in that placement were wrong. Every one of them is silent — and every one is either the identity or
unreachable when there is a single external field, which is why they survived.

**If you have only ever run single-field schedules, one of the four still affects you** (the metric
one), and it changes the schedules you get. Bug fixes only: no public function was removed, no
argument order changed, no new setting. Requires Wolfram Language 15.0+, unchanged.

---

## ⚠️ Read before upgrading

**Regenerate any schedule built before this release.** The thermodynamic metric was inflated by
`E[O]²`, including in the one-dimensional case, and it drives both the sampling density and the
K-means placement. Your replica spacing was therefore not the constant-thermodynamic-length spacing
it claimed to be.

**Nothing you have measured becomes wrong.** A schedule is an *input* to a tempering run, not a
result. What was lost is the acceptance uniformity CTL exists to deliver, not the correctness of
any observable computed from a run that used it.

**Multi-field schedules should be regarded as unreliable before this release**, not merely
suboptimal. Two of the four fixes below could hand a replica different physical parameters than the
clustering chose, or discard the off-diagonal structure that is the whole reason to build one
multi-field schedule instead of one schedule per axis.

---

## 1. A replica's field-vector components could be permuted

The replica centers were ordered with

```wl
centers = LexicographicSort[Sort/@centers, NumericalOrder];
```

The intent was to order the *list* of centers. The inner `Sort` sorted the components **inside each
center**. A center is a field vector whose component `i` is the coupling of conjugate observable
`i`, so permuting them hands that replica different physical parameters, with nothing a caller can
see changing.

On length-1 vectors `Sort` is the identity — so single-field schedules were never affected, and the
three multi-field overloads were the only callers that could reach it. There it swapped the
components of any center whose entries were not already ascending, **and only those**, so a
schedule came back with some replicas correct and others carrying permuted couplings. The result is
not even a permutation of the centers the clustering produced.

## 2. The thermodynamic metric was a second moment, not a covariance

`sigmaMat` was assembled from `ExtrapolatedExpectationValue[… O_i*O_j …]`, under a comment reading
`S_{a,b}=b^2Cov(M^a,M^b)`. But that function is a plain MBAR-reweighted expectation with no mean
subtraction, so the matrix held `E[O_i O_j]`.

The metric that governs replica placement is the Fisher information, which for an exponential
family in these natural parameters is the **covariance** of the sufficient statistics.
`E[O_i O_j]` overstates it by `E[O_i]E[O_j]`, and those means are not small.

**This is the one that affects single-field runs.** With one field the error is a smooth positive
inflation of a 1×1 metric, so the schedule still looked reasonable — the ordering was sane, the
spacing was not. With several fields, or with a hamiltonian written in homogeneous `c·O` form where
one conjugate observable carries the whole energy, the mean term dominates and placement stops
tracking fluctuations altogether. The first moments are now interpolated over the same grid through
the same MBAR call, and subtracted.

## 3. The metric's off-diagonal entries were clipped to zero

The same expression floored *every* entry with `Max[0.0, …]`. That is right for a variance and
wrong for a covariance: two anti-correlated observables have a legitimately negative cross term.
Clipping discarded exactly the off-diagonal structure that makes one multi-field schedule better
than one schedule per axis.

Only the diagonal is floored now — a negative variance there is MBAR noise, and zero is the right
floor — while off-diagonals keep their sign. Residual indefiniteness is absorbed downstream, where
`Det` and the quadratic form in `metricDistance` are both already floored at zero. This never fired
with one field, where `E[O²]` cannot be negative.

## 4. Replicas could be placed on a metric extrapolated from no data

The metric is interpolated over the **bounding box** of the bootstrap field table, and a box is not
the region a bootstrap run actually covers. Corners get reached by extrapolation — the function
really is called `ExtrapolatedExpectationValue` — and a metric invented there can attract replicas
to parameters nothing was ever sampled at.

The replica density is now masked by the **Kish effective sample size** of the MBAR reweighting at
each point, `(Σu)²/Σu²` over the per-sample weights. Below 1% of the total sample count the density
is zero. This is a hard restriction of the domain rather than a reweighting: inside the supported
region the density is exactly `sqrt(det g)^pSoft` as before, so the spacing stays
constant-thermodynamic-length. Weighting *by* the effective sample size would have tilted the
schedule toward well-sampled regions and stopped being CTL.

The mask is geometry-free, which matters because the region that needs excluding depends on how the
hamiltonian is parameterized — for an ordinary multi-field run it is the unvisited corners of a box;
for a hamiltonian in homogeneous `c·O` form it is everything outside a fan through the origin.

Measured on a 12-field, 1200-sample synthetic reweighting: effective sample size runs 127–302
across the sampled range and collapses to **1.0** immediately outside it, against a floor of 12. The
computation is done in log space with a maximum shift, since the weights it is asked about are
precisely the ones whose exponents spread furthest.

## How this was verified

- **The metric fix** is checked against a population covariance computed directly from samples,
  sharing no code with the function under test, on a fixture whose means are nonzero *and* whose
  cross second moment is negative — so one case exercises defects 2 and 3 together. With the old
  semantics restored, four of the test's six rows fail.
- **The effective sample size** is checked against a direct, non-logarithmic Kish computation; the
  two agree to 1e-13 across the sampled range and out to a target 10⁵ away. It returns exactly the
  sample count when all weights are equal, and is bounded by 1 and the sample count elsewhere.
- **Defect 1** is fixed by deletion and is deliberately untested: `centers` is a `Module` local, so
  reaching it needs a full schedule run, and a unit test would have asserted only that
  `LexicographicSort` does not permute sublists. A comment at each of the three sites is the guard.
- A new test pins that `GraphSweepReplica` reads `beta` **only** as the product `beta*delH`, so a
  hamiltonian rescaled by β at `bt = 1` produces a bit-identical chain. That is what makes it valid
  to temper in temperature and external field simultaneously by writing the hamiltonian in
  homogeneous `c·O` form and letting β be the coupling conjugate to the energy.

The suite is at **193 tests**, all passing.

---

## Also noted, not changed

`LogSumExp` shifts by the **mean** rather than the maximum. The two are mathematically identical,
but a mean shift does not survive a wide spread of exponents — the largest term can still overflow.
Nothing in the package currently feeds it such a spread; the effective-sample-size computation added
here shifts by the maximum locally rather than relying on it.
