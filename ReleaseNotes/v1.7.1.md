# ECGrav 1.7.1 — the ground-state search reported minima that were not minima

`GraphCTLSchedule` returned per-replica minimum energies that were not the minimum: states the
run had visited, recorded, and handed back in its own output had **lower** energy than the
minimum it reported. Sometimes the accompanying `minEstates` was empty, and the reported number
was the `minEToBeat` threshold — an energy no visited state ever had.

The cause was two levels below where it looked. `GraphMultiHistogram`'s bookkeeping was correct;
`GraphSweepReplica` weighed a state's energy against its running minimum **only inside
`If[accept==1,...]`**, so a state could enter the minimum only by being *stepped into* by an
accepted move. The state a sweep **starts from** was never weighed — and that is the state the
chain occupies through every rejected move.

Seeded on K6, the ground state of `h[-1.,0.]`, at beta 5 where every move out costs `+8` and
nothing is ever accepted, the sweep sat at `-60.` for all 120 steps and reported `-50.` when
asked to beat `-50.`, with no states to show for it.

Bug fixes only. **No public function was removed, no argument order changed, no new setting.**

---

## ⚠️ Read before upgrading

**Ground-state results from 1.7.0 and earlier are wrong in a specific, checkable way.** If you
have recorded minima from `GraphCTLSchedule`, `GraphMultiHistogram`, `GraphParallelTempering` or
`GraphComputeCorrelationTime`, the reported minimum may sit above energies those runs actually
visited, and `minEstates` may be empty or missing degenerate states. Runs where the chain reached
its minimum by an accepted move and stayed active are unaffected. Re-running is the only way to
recover the ones that are not.

**Energies can differ from 1.7.0 in their last bits.** Reported energies are now recomputed from
the hamiltonian rather than carried as incremental accumulations, so charted energies, specific
heats and free energies shift at the 10⁻¹⁵ level. Seeded runs still agree at any tolerance you
would use physically.

---

## What changed

**A sweep now weighs the state it starts from.** Both `GraphSweepReplica` overloads. This is what
the function's own documentation already promised — "the minimum energy visited throughout the
sweep" — and it is the idiom `SGradDescent` twenty lines above it already used.

**Replica swaps no longer hide configurations from the receiving replica.** A swap moves a
configuration into a replica without any accepted move, and under that replica's external field
its energy is a number no sweep has weighed. Fixing the sweep covers every such configuration,
since each one seeds the receiving replica's next sweep. The configuration held after the *final*
swap is not swept again, so the six drivers that track minima per replica — `GraphMultiHistogram`
and `GraphParallelTempering`, external-field overloads, with and without `delH` — now collect it
when the measurement loop ends, recomputing its energy under that replica's own field.

The beta-swap drivers were never affected: they keep one global minimum under a single
hamiltonian, so a swap moves no energy they had not already seen.

**`GraphComputeCorrelationTime` inherited the hole directly** — it starts its running minimum *at*
`minEToBeat` with no states and relies entirely on the sweep to improve it, so a frozen chain
returned the threshold. `GraphEquilibriate` never had the bug; it initialises from
`hamiltonian[seedGraph]`.

**Reported energies are the hamiltonian's own values.** The sweep builds energy incrementally and
drifts a few ulps. On machine reals `==` and `<` share one *relative* tolerance and never both
hold, so that drift is normally invisible — but a relative tolerance has nothing to scale at zero.
For a hamiltonian whose ground state sits at exactly `0.`, the sweep reported minima such as
`-4.163336342344337*^-16`: a minimum **below the least energy the model can produce**, which then
compares unequal to `0.` and drops the states tying it. Both sweep overloads now recompute the
reported minimum, and the returned state's energy, once the step loop ends.

## Performance

**Unchanged.** The canonicalisation above was first implemented inside the step loop and measured
at **+51%** (beta 1) and **+24%** (beta 6) — the hamiltonian is a matrix product where a step is a
row sum through `delH`, 25–50× dearer — so it was moved to two calls per sweep instead of one per
step. The sweep's step loop is byte-for-byte what it was in 1.7.0. `SGradDescent` and
`SimulatedAnnealing` canonicalise in-loop, which measured within noise there because each of their
steps already scans the whole edge list.

## Testing

**175 tests, all passing** (`wolframscript -file Tests/ci-run.wls`, ~70 s).

Three are new, and all three fail against the 1.7.0 source. None pins an RNG stream: two freeze
the chain *by physics* rather than by seed — K4 is the exact ground state of `h[-1.,0.]` and every
move out costs `+4`, so at beta 10 the acceptance weight is `Exp[-40]` and the expected energy is
a hand-computed constant. The third asserts bit-for-bit agreement with `Order[a,b]==0`, because
`==`, `===` and the suite's own `floatEq` are all tolerant on machine reals and pass on precisely
the values it exists to reject.

The `GraphMultiHistogram` and `GraphParallelTempering` invariant tests now also demand a non-empty
`minEstates` and assert that the reported minimum is at or below every energy the run recorded in
its own chart. `groundStatesConsistentQ` passes vacuously on an empty `minEstates` — which is
exactly what this bug produced, and why the suite did not catch it.
[`Tests/README.md`](https://github.com/kassabetre/ECGrav/blob/main/Tests/README.md) documents both
traps.

One branch is unproven and labelled as such: the strict-lowering branch of the new end-of-loop
update has not been observed firing. Its tie branch was measured firing in an instrumented run.

Download `ECGrav-1.7.1.paclet` below, then:

```wl
PacletInstall["/path/to/ECGrav-1.7.1.paclet"]
```

Requires Wolfram Language 15.0+, unchanged from 1.7.0.
