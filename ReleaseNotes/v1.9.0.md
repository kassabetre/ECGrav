# ECGrav 1.9.0 — the graph correlation time catches up with its complex-space twin

`GraphComputeCorrelationTime` computed every lag of its autocorrelation table and then threw most
of them away. The integral stops at the first non-positive term — usually after a handful of lags
— but all `numsweeps-10` of them were tabulated first, about 990 at the default cap. Each costs
O(run length), so the measurement was **quadratic in the run length**, and raising
`$ECGravMaxCorrelationSweeps` cost as its square.

Its complex-space twin, `RandomPureSimplicialComplexMCMCCorrelationTime`, was rewritten in 1.7.0.
This brings the four graph overloads up to it, together with the three defects the complex side
had already had fixed.

**No public function was removed and no argument order changed.** One key is added to the
correlation-time results. Requires Wolfram Language 15.0+, unchanged.

---

## ⚠️ Read before upgrading

**Every correlation time that previously computed is unchanged**, so there is nothing to re-run
and no past result to distrust on that account.

Two things do change, both of them cases that previously produced no usable number at all:

- **`corrT` used to come back as an unevaluated expression on short runs.** Any call with
  `eqlT <= 1` hit this, in all four overloads. If you have code that silently carried a symbolic
  `Max[...]` where an integer belonged, it will now get a `2`.
- **The overload taking an operator list and no delH** returned unevaluated `corrT` and
  `corrTValues` whenever any observable was constant. Reachable from `GraphMultiHistogram` and
  `GraphParallelTempering` in their no-delH external-field forms. A constant observable is easier
  to write than it looks — `Length[g]` is one, since the vertex count does not change during a
  sweep.

If you index the returned association **by position** rather than by key, note that
`corrTMeasured` is now among its keys. Indexing by key, as the drivers do, is unaffected.

## What changed

### The lag table is walked, not tabulated

A new internal routine walks the lags, stops at the turnover, and then extends the curve —
without summing — over exactly the window the diagnostic plot displays, and no further.

Both conventions of the old tabulated form are preserved deliberately, because they set the
answer: the terminating non-positive term **is** the last term of the integral, and an
autocorrelation that never turns over stops one lag short of the range.

Measured on the correlation step alone:

| Run length | Uncorrelated | Strongly autocorrelated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 000 sweeps | 54x | 8x |
| 5 000 sweeps | 155x | 20x |
| 20 000 sweeps | 581x | 70x |

End-to-end wall clock at the **default** cap barely moves — the sweep loop dominates there. The
gain is what makes a raised cap practical.

One case does not gain: an observable whose autocorrelation never turns over at all — a drifting
or monotone one — still walks the whole range, at about 20–25% more overhead per lag than the
vectorised tabulation it replaced. That is a flat constant, not a growing one (0.75x at 1 000
sweeps, 0.81x at 10 000).

### `corrTMeasured` — telling a measured correlation time from a defaulted one

`corrT` falls back to a floor of 2 in two situations that left no other trace: every observable
frozen, and a run too short to have a lag range at all. Neither was distinguishable from a chain
that genuinely decorrelates in two sweeps by looking at `corrT`, and the second is reported by no
message. Since `corrT` is the sweep count the drivers use between measurements, callers had no
way to tell an interval that means something from one that means nothing.

Every correlation-time result now carries `corrTMeasured`, on both the graph and complex sides,
and it is carried through `RandomPureSimplicialComplexMCMC`:

```wl
ct = GraphComputeCorrelationTime[seed, beta, h[-1., 0.], dH[-1., 0.], eqlT, 0., 1, 0];

ct[[2, "corrT"]]           (* 2 *)
ct[[2, "corrTMeasured"]]   (* False — the 2 is a default, not a measurement *)
```

**This is a flag rather than a larger default, deliberately.** `corrT` multiplies the cost of
every production run, so inflating it for a stuck chain would make the runs that froze the most
expensive ones — while still producing identical samples. And at high beta a frozen chain usually
means the ground state was **found**, which is the intended outcome rather than a failure. What to
do about an unmeasured correlation time is the caller's judgement.

A single stuck observable alongside a live one does **not** clear the flag: `corrT` is the maximum
over the observables that do fluctuate.

### Three defects fixed

- **`corrT` returned unevaluated on short runs**, in all four overloads. The integration cut-off
  came from `FirstPosition[..., numsweeps-10][[1]]`, whose default was a bare integer rather than
  a position list, so whenever the autocorrelation never went non-positive that indexed an
  integer. Every run with `numsweeps < 10` does, its lag table being empty.
- **The operator-list overload without a delH had no stuck-observable handling at all** — a frozen
  observable put a `Null` in the table, which indexed its way into `corrT`. It now filters frozen
  observables, reports `::stuck` / `::alldefault` / `::shortrun`, sizes `corrTValues` per
  observable, and draws its autocorrelation plot, matching the overload that takes a delH.
- **`::stuck` reported the wrong value** — it named one observable and printed the value of the
  one two rows above it. The plot legend had the matching defect: it was built from all
  observables while only the fluctuating ones get a curve, so anything stuck shifted every label
  onto the wrong line.

## How this was verified

Correlation times are easy to change by accident, so equivalence was established three ways:

- **147 synthetic AR(1) series** (rho 0 → 0.99, correlation times up to 82) agree with the old
  tabulated form on the correlation time, on the integration cut-off, **and** on the plotted
  curve — term for term. A negative control that integrates the whole table separates the
  conventions in 72 of the 147, so the agreement is not vacuous.
- **96 end-to-end configurations** across all four overloads: 400 of 400 exact matches on every
  value the old code was able to produce, and the 176 it could not are now clean numbers.
- **The new tests were checked against the old code**: all of them fail on it, and every
  pre-existing test still passes.

The suite is at **185 tests**, all passing.

```wl
PacletInstall["/path/to/ECGrav-1.9.0.paclet"]
```
